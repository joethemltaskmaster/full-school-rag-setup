INSTITUTIONAL OPERATIONAL FRAMEWORK: SCHOOL POLICY, ATTENDANCE HANDBOOK, INTERVENTION GUIDE & COUNSELLING PROCEDURES

SECTION 1: INSTITUTIONAL SCHOOL POLICY

1.1 Academic & Termly Governance

* Term Structure: The academic session operates on a 13-week termly structure split across three terms (First Term: September–December; Second Term: January–April; Third Term: April–July).
* Instructional Hours: The operational school day runs from 07:45 AM to 03:30 PM, Monday through Friday. A standard day is partitioned into 6 instructional periods of 40 minutes each, alongside morning assembly (20 minutes), mid-morning short break (20 minutes), and long lunch break (45 minutes).
* Class Arm Organization: Classes are structured sequentially from Junior Secondary (JSS1–JSS3) to Senior Secondary (SS1–SS3), partitioned into distinct arm designations (e.g., SS3A, SS3B). Class arm sizes must not exceed 35 students per arm for general subjects and 25 students per arm for technical/laboratory sessions.

1.2 Faculty Roles & Persona-Based Operational Guidelines

To balance operational efficiency with human retention, academic workload distribution is governed by persona-specific operational constraints:

Staff Persona Category: Overworked Generalist
Contractual Profile: Full-time staff assigned across multiple lower/middle school subjects (e.g., PHE, Basic Tech, CRS).
Daily/Weekly Max Load: Max 5 periods/day; Max 22 periods/week.
Specific Scheduling Policy & Constraints: Mandatory 1-period rest buffer after 3 consecutive periods. Core morning slots preferred; total consecutive teaching must never exceed 3 periods in afternoon sessions.

Staff Persona Category: Hustling Subject Specialist
Contractual Profile: Part-time / Multi-school specialist (e.g., Physics, Further Math).
Daily/Weekly Max Load: Max 3 periods/day; Contracted morning windows.
Specific Scheduling Policy & Constraints: Strictly scheduled within fixed window (08:00 AM – 11:00 AM). Must be granted zero afternoon periods on days with cross-city travel demands to accommodate urban transit delays.

Staff Persona Category: NYSC Corper
Contractual Profile: National Youth Service Corps primary assignment staff.
Daily/Weekly Max Load: Max 3 periods/day; Max 14 periods/week.
Specific Scheduling Policy & Constraints: Zero lecture allocation on designated CDS (Community Development Service) days. Assigned single or double period blocks; strictly prohibited from handling final-period senior exam prep arms without co-teacher oversight.

Staff Persona Category: Veteran Staff / Room Boss
Contractual Profile: Senior full-time staff / HODs with high tenure.
Daily/Weekly Max Load: Max 4 periods/day; Max 16 periods/week.
Specific Scheduling Policy & Constraints: Priority morning placement (08:30 AM – 10:30 AM). Assigned fixed master classrooms; exempt from floating across junior arms unless conducting supervisory audits.

1.3 Infrastructure & Facility Usage Policy

* Laboratory & Specialized Room Matching: Science subjects (Physics, Chemistry, Biology), Computer Studies, and Technical Drawing must hold practical sessions in designated laboratory facilities. Holding practical lectures in standard homeroom spaces constitutes a Class A Infrastructure Breakdown.
* Power & Generator Synchronized Scheduling: Computer Studies, Audio-Visual, and Science Laboratory sessions requiring heavy electrification must be clustered during scheduled generator power windows (09:30 AM – 01:30 PM) to optimize fuel efficiency and prevent lesson abandonment due to power outages.
* Room Capacity Enforcement: Floating classes must not exceed the physical seating capacity of specialized laboratories. Over-capacity transfers require prior written sign-off from the Vice Principal (Academics).

SECTION 2: COMPREHENSIVE ATTENDANCE HANDBOOK

2.1 Student Attendance Framework

* Mandatory Attendance Threshold: Students are required to maintain a minimum of 85% verified attendance across all scheduled subject periods per term to remain eligible for end-of-term examinations and external registration (BECE, WAEC, NECO).
* Daily Roll Call Procedure:
1. Morning Assembly Registration (07:45 AM – 08:05 AM): Form Tutors execute physical attendance logging in the Master Class Register.
2. Period-by-Period Audit: Class Captains maintain a physical Period Logbook. Subject teachers sign the logbook at the start and end of every period.
3. Digital Reconciliation: Form Tutors transcribe daily physical logs into the school database by 02:00 PM daily.



Daily Attendance Reconciliation Pipeline:
07:45 AM: Assembly Roll Call (Form Tutor)
->
Period 1–6: Class Captain Logbook Entry & Teacher Signature
->
02:00 PM: Digital System Entry & Discrepancy Flagging
->
03:30 PM: Automated SMS Notification to Parents of Absentee Students

* Absence Classification:
* Authorized Absence: Medical illness (supported by a licensed clinic medical certificate), family bereavement, or officially sanctioned inter-school competitions.
* Unauthorized Absence (Truancy): Unexcused non-attendance, skipping specific subject periods while present on school grounds, or arriving 20+ minutes late without a pass.



2.2 Faculty Attendance, Punctuality & Mobility Management

* Clock-In Mandate: All full-time faculty must physically or digitally clock in before 07:30 AM. Part-time specialists must clock in at least 15 minutes prior to their contracted period window.
* Period Attendance Verification: A teacher is marked as Present and Active when they enter the assigned room within 5 minutes of the period bell. Arriving 10+ minutes late constitutes Unexcused Tardiness.
* Off-Site & CDS Tracking: NYSC Corpers must submit their official Local Government CDS clearance schedules at the start of each term. Absence from school on non-CDS days without an approved leave request is treated as an unexcused absence, resulting in formal allowance deductions.

SECTION 3: RISK-BASED INTERVENTION GUIDE

3.1 Multi-Tiered Intervention System (MTIS)

To prevent operational breakdown, schedule friction, and student absenteeism, the institution uses a 3-tier risk-scoring model based on predictive indicators (e.g., teacher exhaustion score, soft preference violation, consecutive teaching load, travel overlap).

Tier Structure:

* TIER 3: CRITICAL INTERVENTION (Risk Score > 0.50)
* Unexcused Period Skips, CDS Clashes, Room Mismatches
* Immediate Admin Override & Formal Disciplinary Review


* TIER 2: EARLY WARNING (Risk Score 0.20 – 0.50)
* Fatigue Accumulation (4+ periods), Minor Tardiness
* HOD Timetable Adjustment & Proactive Wellness Check


* TIER 1: ROUTINE MONITORING (Risk Score < 0.20)
* Standard Operational Execution & Routine Automated Tracking



3.2 Incident Escalation Matrix

Trigger Condition: Soft Preference Score Dropping Below -4.0
Risk Level: Tier 2
Responsible Authority: HOD / Timetable Committee
Turnaround Window: 24 Hours
Required Action: Re-evaluate scheduling parameters; adjust consecutive period counts or shift late afternoon slots to morning windows.

Trigger Condition: Consecutive Periods >= 4 for Overworked Generalist
Risk Level: Tier 2
Responsible Authority: Vice Principal (Academics)
Turnaround Window: Same Day
Required Action: Assign an Assistant Teacher or Intern to period 3 or 4 to share the teaching load; force a 40-minute rest buffer.

Trigger Condition: NYSC Corper Scheduled on CDS Blocked Day
Risk Level: Tier 3
Responsible Authority: Timetable Admin / Core Optimizer
Turnaround Window: Immediate (<2 Hours)
Required Action: Hard Constraint Override: Instantly remove Corper from schedule on CDS day; reallocate load to a floating generalist.

Trigger Condition: Unexcused Teacher Absence / Skipping Period
Risk Level: Tier 3
Responsible Authority: Principal / Disciplinary Panel
Turnaround Window: 4 Hours
Required Action: Deploy emergency internal substitute cover; issue formal query to teacher; log incident in termly performance file.

Trigger Condition: Student Class-Skipping (Subject Truancy)
Risk Level: Tier 2
Responsible Authority: Guidance Counsellor / Form Tutor
Turnaround Window: 12 Hours
Required Action: Issue Student Warning Pass; notify parents via SMS; schedule compulsory counselling session before next subject class.

Trigger Condition: Student Cumulative Attendance < 85%
Risk Level: Tier 3
Responsible Authority: VP Academics / Parent Board
Turnaround Window: 48 Hours
Required Action: Issue formal Examination Barring Warning; mandatory parent-principal conference; place student on Academic Probation.

3.3 Operational Protocol for Schedule Friction

1. Detection: The system flags a high risk score (>= 0.50) or a physical clash occurs during daily execution.
2. Containment: The HOD immediately pulls from the Floating Substitute Pool (unassigned generalists or intern teachers) to ensure the classroom is supervised and instructed.
3. Root-Cause Remediation:
* If caused by transit delays (Part-time specialist), shift the specialist’s classes to a single-block morning setup.
* If caused by facility conflict (e.g., dual lab booking), move the non-practical component of the curriculum to the homeroom and reschedule the practical lab for off-peak hours.



SECTION 4: COUNSELLING & SUPPORT PROCEDURES

4.1 Faculty Wellness & Burnout Mitigation

* Burnout Screening: Faculty members identified with continuous high exhaustion indices (e.g., teaching 20+ periods/week across 3+ subjects) undergo mandatory bi-weekly wellness evaluations by the Guidance and Counselling Department.
* Workload Re-balancing: Upon counsellor recommendation, the Vice Principal (Academics) must reassign up to 20% of the affected teacher's load to intern teachers or re-adjust class arm allocations.
* De-escalation & Mental Health Support: Teachers experiencing acute stress, personal crises, or burnout are granted up to 3 days of paid compassionate wellness leave, supported by confidential professional counselling resources.

4.2 Student Academic & Behavioral Counselling

* Truancy & Discipline Intake: Students flagged for Tier 2 or Tier 3 attendance infractions are referred to the Counselling Unit rather than subjected strictly to punitive measures.

Student Behavioral Counselling Workflow:
Attendance Flag (Infraction logged)
->
Phase 1: Initial Diagnostic Intake (Identify root causes: home environment, learning difficulty, peer pressure)
->
Phase 2: Individualized Behavioral Plan (IBP) (Set attendance targets, daily check-ins with Counsellor)
->
Phase 3: Parental Alignment Conference (Formulate home-school compliance agreement)
->
Phase 4: Bi-Weekly Monitoring & Progress Sign-Off (Minimum 4 weeks)

* Career & Academic Guidance: Senior secondary students (SS1–SS3) receive structured counseling regarding subject selection (Sciences, Arts, Commercial), study habits, and national exam preparation (WAEC/NECO/UTME) to reduce academic anxiety.

4.3 Conflict Mediation & Grievance Standards

* Teacher-Administration Disputes: Disputes regarding timetable placements, room allocations, or unfair workload distributions must be lodged in writing using the Staff Grievance Form.
* Mediation Process:
1. Stage 1 (Informal): Direct dialogue between Teacher and HOD within 48 hours.
2. Stage 2 (Formal Mediation): Meeting convened by the Guidance Counsellor, Vice Principal, and affected staff member within 3 business days.
3. Stage 3 (Final Resolution): The Principal issues a binding operational decision that balances teacher well-being with institutional integrity.


* Confidentiality Standard: All counselling records, psychological evaluations, and grievance proceedings must be kept strictly confidential in locked physical vaults and encrypted digital directories accessible only by authorized personnel.

KNOWLEDGE BASE MODULE: STUDENT DROPOUT RISK CATEGORIZATION & EARLY WARNING FRAMEWORK

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & OPERATIONAL SCOPE

This module establishes a standardized, multi-indicator Dropout Risk Categorization System designed to identify at-risk students well before academic collapse or permanent withdrawal occurs. By combining quantitative academic and attendance telemetries with qualitative socio-economic and behavioral indicators, the framework partitions students into three actionable risk tiers: Low Risk (Tier 1), Medium Risk (Tier 2), and High Risk (Tier 3).

2. RISK CATEGORIZATION MATRIX & MULTI-FACTOR CRITERIA

LOW RISK (Tier 1: Green)
Composite Risk Score: 0.00 - 0.24
Primary Indicator Criteria:
- Attendance >= 90%
- Academic Average >= 60%
- Zero core subject failures
- Timely fee settlement
Operational Thresholds: Standard operational stability; high engagement in extracurriculars; zero major behavioral flags.
Immediate System Action: Routine Termly Tracking & Positive Reinforcement.

MEDIUM RISK (Tier 2: Yellow)
Composite Risk Score: 0.25 - 0.55
Primary Indicator Criteria:
- Attendance 75% - 89%
- Academic Average 45% - 59%
- Failing 1-2 core subjects
- Delayed fee payments
Operational Thresholds: Chronic tardiness, periodic subject skipping, mild behavioral withdrawal, emerging family/economic stress.
Immediate System Action: Tier 2 Targeted Intervention - Form Tutor audit, mandatory academic tutoring, parent-teacher alignment meeting within 5 days.

HIGH RISK (Tier 3: Red)
Composite Risk Score: 0.56 - 1.00
Primary Indicator Criteria:
- Attendance < 75%
- Academic Average < 45%
- Failing 3+ subjects
- Severe fee arrears
Operational Thresholds: Unexcused absences >= 10 days, severe behavioral violations, child labor/economic distress, family crisis.
Immediate System Action: Tier 3 Emergency Intervention - Immediate principal & counsellor conference, individualized retention plan, financial relief routing.

3. DEEP-DIVE RISK PROFILE BREAKDOWN

3.1 Low Risk Profile (Tier 1 - Preventive Maintenance)

Academic Telemetry: Student consistently achieves passing or credit-level grades across all core (Mathematics, English Language) and elective subjects. Term-over-term trajectory is stable or positive.

Attendance & Punctuality: Student logs >= 90% verified attendance. Lateness is rare (< 3 instances per term) and supported by valid parental excuses.

Psychosocial & Economic Indicators: Active involvement in classroom activities, sports, or clubs. School fees and resource requirements (textbooks, uniforms, lab coats) are fully settled within the first three weeks of the term.

Management Protocol: Subjected to standard automated termly logging. Form Tutors provide quarterly commendations to sustain engagement.

3.2 Medium Risk Profile (Tier 2 - Vulnerable / Early Warning)

Academic Telemetry: Terminal score averages fluctuate between 45% and 59%, or the student fails one critical core gateway subject (e.g., Mathematics or English Language). Homework completion rate drops below 70%.

Attendance & Punctuality: Attendance drops into the 75% - 89% band. Patterns exhibit "soft truancy" (e.g., missing Mondays or Fridays, late arrival after long break, or skipping specific afternoon periods).

Psychosocial & Economic Indicators: Noticeable emotional withdrawal, increased peer friction, or mild behavioral infractions (e.g., dress code violations, unsubmitted assignments). Tuition payment extends beyond mid-term break, signaling household financial strain.

Management Protocol:
1. Trigger automated notification to Form Tutor and Guidance Counsellor.
2. Conduct a Diagnostic Intake Interview to identify underlying bottlenecks (e.g., learning difficulty, transportation issues, domestic chores).
3. Enroll student in Subject Remediation & Peer Tutoring.

3.3 High Risk Profile (Tier 3 - Critical / Imminent Withdrawal)

Academic Telemetry: Aggregate academic score falls below 45%, with failures in three or more subjects across two consecutive terms. The student exhibits severe learning gaps and test anxiety.

Attendance & Punctuality: Attendance falls below 75% or logs 10+ cumulative unexcused absences. Unexplained disappearance for 3+ consecutive days without parental communication.

Psychosocial & Economic Indicators: Severe behavioral disruption, acute emotional distress, or complete disengagement from school culture. Severe fee defaults spanning multiple terms, indications of child labor demands (e.g., trading/hawking during school hours), or major domestic disruption (e.g., loss of primary breadwinner, severe illness in family).

Management Protocol:
1. Issue immediate Tier 3 Critical Risk Alert to Vice Principal (Academics), School Counsellor, and Principal.
2. Mandate a formal Case Conference with parents/guardians within 48 hours.
3. Deploy a Customized Student Retention Agreement (CSRA), incorporating academic adjustments, flexible tuition payment structures, or referral to institutional welfare/scholarship funds.

4. QUANTITATIVE COMPOSITE RISK SCORE (CRS) ENGINE

To eliminate subjective bias, the system computes a numerical Composite Risk Score (CRS) for every student using a weighted multi-variable evaluation formula:

CRS = (wA * SA) + (wP * SP) + (wF * SF) + (wB * SB)

Where variables and weights (wi) are defined as follows:

Attendance Deficit Index (SA) - Weight 0.35
Score Mapping: SA = 1.0 - (Actual Attendance / Required Attendance). Values clipped to [0, 1].

Academic Failure Index (SP) - Weight 0.30
Score Mapping: Proportion of failed subjects relative to total registered subjects.

Fee Settlement Deficit (SF) - Weight 0.20
Score Mapping: 0.0 if fully paid on time; 0.5 if paid post mid-term; 1.0 if defaulting across terms.

Behavioral & Life Event Flag (SB) - Weight 0.15
Score Mapping: Cumulative score derived from logged disciplinary infractions, bereavement, or sudden trauma.

Decision Boundary Mapping:
- CRS < 0.25 -> Low Risk (Tier 1)
- 0.25 <= CRS <= 0.55 -> Medium Risk (Tier 2)
- CRS > 0.55 -> High Risk (Tier 3)

5. ESCALATION & INTERVENTION WORKFLOW

STUDENT TELEMETRY LOGGED DAILY
(Attendance, Assessment Grades, Fee Logs, Behavioral Incidents)
   -> COMPOSITE RISK SCORE (CRS) ENGINE
        -> CRS < 0.25 [TIER 1, Low Risk]: Routine Termly Tracking
        -> 0.25 <= CRS <= 0.55 [TIER 2, Medium Risk]: Form Tutor Interview; Remedial Class; Parent SMS
        -> CRS > 0.55 [TIER 3, High Risk]: Mandatory Parent-Principal Conference (48 hrs); Custom Retention Agreement; Welfare/Fee Relief Route

6. DE-ESCALATION & EXIT STANDARDS

A student assigned to Tier 2 or Tier 3 cannot be reclassified to a lower risk tier until they meet the following exit criteria across a minimum monitoring period:

Medium to Low Risk Transition:
- Attendance sustained at >= 85% for 6 consecutive weeks.
- Passing grades achieved in all failed core subjects during mid-term or end-of-term evaluations.
- Zero unexcused tardiness or disciplinary infractions over the monitoring window.

High to Medium Risk Transition:
- Attendance restored to >= 75% with no unexcused absences for 4 consecutive weeks.
- Re-engagement in learning confirmed by Form Tutor and Guidance Counsellor via signed weekly progress reports.
- Active execution of a binding fee workout plan or scholarship support arrangement.
